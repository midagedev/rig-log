# GLM-5.3-Flash on this machine: first numbers, and a decode that is not bandwidth-bound

**2026-09-15, 06:54–08:10.** Two windows: placement, then the two edges
(more layers than fit, and no GPU at all) with the served model's perplexity
measured the same way for comparison.

## Why this model, and not GLM-5.3

Everything measured in this log so far is DeepSeek. A second MoE family on
the same probes tells whether the rules found on V4/V4.1 (bandwidth-bound
decode, cards free to read, the ubatch worth 2×) are about this hardware or
about that architecture. GLM-5.3-Flash (zai-org, 2026-08-25, MIT) is 320 B
total / 18 B active, 288 routed experts top-8, 45 layers of which 34 are KDA
linear attention and 11 are MLA with a DeepSeek-style sparse-attention
indexer and a k-pool, plus one MTP layer and a vision tower. The full
GLM-5.3 (753 B, about 40 B active) was rejected on arithmetic: only ≤2-bit
files clear 251 GB of RAM and the decode ceiling at 145 GB/s is ~11 tok/s,
below what the machine already serves.

Support state on the day (research round, `gh` read-only): ggml-org master
has no `glm5next`; three PRs (#27752, #27754 unsloth, #27773) are open and
conflicting, maintainer attention on #27773, which needs a rewritten
shard 1 to load the published GGUF. ik_llama.cpp merged its port on
2026-09-14 (PR #2376) and its author validated it on the same unsloth file,
so ik main is the first build; #27773 is the mainline comparison to do
second. MTP is a separate open PR on both sides.

## Setup

- File: `unsloth/GLM-5.3-Flash-GGUF` UD-Q4_K_XL, six shards, 186 GiB,
  fetched with the per-shard guard script (flock, size cap, sha256 against
  the LFS oid, `/tmp/rig-quiet` pause), 06:54–07:42 at ~70 MB/s.
- Engine: ik_llama.cpp upstream main 7434a014 (2026-09-14), built in its own
  worktree so the serving build is untouched; CUDA arch 86.
- Flags from the PR threads, not the model card: `NVIDIA_TF32_OVERRIDE=0`
  (the CUDA backend enables TF32 unconditionally; top-1 agreement with the
  reference was 0.896 without it), `-fa on`, `--jinja --reasoning-format
  deepseek --chat-template-kwargs '{"reasoning_effort":"high"}'` (reasoning
  cannot be turned off, only sized), `-c 32768 -b 4096 -ub 4096 -t 32`,
  routed experts on the CPU with `-ot exps=CPU` and layers pinned to the
  cards with further `-ot` rules. No draft model.
- Probes are the serving ones: perplexity on four wikitext-2 chunks at
  c 2048, three 400-token greedy decodes, one 11 429-token prefill (a 50 000
  character slice of the same text), VRAM before and after. `llm.service`
  stopped, GPUs otherwise idle.

## What the numbers say so far

**The decode is not bandwidth-bound.** Each layer of routed experts moved
from RAM to a card removes 1/45 of the expert bytes a token reads, about
2.2 %; the measured gain is about 1 % a layer (17.0 → 18.2 for eight layers,
→ 18.7 for ten). On V4-Flash the same move paid in full. At 145 GB/s the
expert bytes alone (8 of 288 experts a layer, 177 GB of experts) allow
about 30 tok/s; the machine delivers 17–19, so roughly half of each decode
step is somewhere other than the RAM read — the KDA chunked scan on 34
layers, the indexer, or per-step overhead are the candidates, and a profile
of one decode step is the next measurement. This is the first architecture
on which the "cards are free, RAM is the whole cost" rule from V4 does not
hold, which is exactly what a second family was for.

**Against the served model.** V4-Flash serves at 26 tok/s with the DSpark
draft and measured 22 without one on the same probes; GLM-5.3-Flash without
a draft is 18.7, 15 % behind at the same quantization family. The
equivalent lever here is the MTP layer (ik PR #2399 reports 1.43× on a
CPU-only box), not yet built.

**Thirteen layers is one too many for `-ub 4096`.** Decode reached 19.6,
then the 11.4k prefill died on the 24 GB card in `ggml_cuda_pool_vmm::alloc`
— the run-time pool for the ubatch had no room next to three expert layers
and the load-time buffers (20.1 GB before the prefill began) — and the
server aborted rather than failing the request, the same class as the V4.1
fork's `-ub 4096` abort (mainline #28338). On this box the ubatch and the
third card-resident layer on the 24 GB card are alternatives; ten layers
(seven plus three) is the standing placement at `-ub 4096`, and the pool
sits at 32.3 / 23.1 GB after a long prefill.

**Without the cards, 8.3 tok/s.** `-ngl 0` puts every weight in RAM (193 GB
resident), and decode lands where the arithmetic said it would: the
attention, shared-expert and head tensors add about 8.5 GB a token to the
4.9 GB of routed experts, and 13.4 GB a token at 145 GB/s is an 11 tok/s
ceiling. Prefill stayed at 283 tok/s because the CUDA backend still runs
large batched matmuls on the cards at `-ngl 0` (9.3 GB of VRAM in use after
the prefill), so a CPU-only prefill was not measured; a pure-CPU number
needs the build without CUDA, which is a different binary.

**Perplexity is not comparable across the two models** — different
tokenizers make "per token" a different unit — so 2.04 against the served
V4-Flash's 2.94 on the same text says nothing about which model is better;
the V4-Flash row is here so that the next GLM file (a different
quantization, or a grafted one) has a same-method baseline, and because the
served model's four-chunk figure had not been recorded before.

**Prefill** at 353 tok/s is 70 % of V4-Flash's 500 on the same ubatch and
the same expert placement (seven plus four there, seven plus three here).

## Things checked and not reported

The loader prints `buffer type overridden` lines for the MTP layer's
expert tensors (`blk.45`) and then reports 29 skipped tensors, 4 367 MiB.
Read against the loader source, the 29 tensors are that layer: it is
skipped whole when MTP is off and the override lines print before the
skip. No RAM is wasted; the log line is merely misleading.
