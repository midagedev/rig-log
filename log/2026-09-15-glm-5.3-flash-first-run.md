# GLM-5.3-Flash on this machine: first numbers, and a decode that is not bandwidth-bound

**2026-09-15, 06:54–08:45.** Four windows: placement; the two edges (more
layers than fit, and no GPU at all) with the served model's perplexity
measured the same way; a thread sweep; and the model's own MTP layer as a
draft, on ik PR #2399 merged onto main.

## Why this model, and not GLM-5.3

Everything measured in this log so far is DeepSeek. A second MoE family on
the same probes tells whether the rules found on V4/V4.1 (bandwidth-bound
decode, cards free to read, the ubatch worth 2×) are about this hardware or
about that architecture. GLM-5.3-Flash (zai-org, 2026-08-25, MIT) is 320 B
total / 18 B active, 288 routed experts top-8, 45 layers of which 34 are KDA
linear attention and 11 are MLA with a DeepSeek-style sparse-attention
indexer and a k-pool, plus one MTP layer and a vision tower. The full
GLM-5.3 (753 B, about 40 B active) was rejected on arithmetic: only ≤2-bit
files clear 251 GB of RAM and the decode ceiling at 145 GB/s is ~11 tok/s,
below what the machine already serves.

Support state on the day (research round, `gh` read-only): ggml-org master
has no `glm5next`; three PRs (#27752, #27754 unsloth, #27773) are open and
conflicting, maintainer attention on #27773, which needs a rewritten
shard 1 to load the published GGUF. ik_llama.cpp merged its port on
2026-09-14 (PR #2376) and its author validated it on the same unsloth file,
so ik main is the first build; #27773 is the mainline comparison to do
second. MTP is a separate open PR on both sides.

- File: `unsloth/GLM-5.3-Flash-GGUF` UD-Q4_K_XL, six shards, 186 GiB,

- Engine: ik_llama.cpp upstream main 7434a014 (2026-09-14), built in its own
  worktree so the serving build is untouched; CUDA arch 86.
- Flags from the PR threads, not the model card: `NVIDIA_TF32_OVERRIDE=0`
  (the CUDA backend enables TF32 unconditionally; top-1 agreement with the
  reference was 0.896 without it), `-fa on`, `--jinja --reasoning-format
  deepseek --chat-template-kwargs '{"reasoning_effort":"high"}'` (reasoning

- Probes are the serving ones: perplexity on four wikitext-2 chunks at

## What the numbers say so far

**The decode is not bandwidth-bound.** Each layer of routed experts moved
from RAM to a card removes 1/45 of the expert bytes a token reads, about
2.2 %; the measured gain is about 1 % a layer (17.0 → 18.2 for eight layers,
→ 18.7 for ten). On V4-Flash the same move paid in full. At 145 GB/s the
expert bytes alone (8 of 288 experts a layer, 177 GB of experts) allow
about 30 tok/s; the machine delivers 17–19, so roughly half of each decode
step is somewhere other than the RAM read — the KDA chunked scan on 34
layers, the indexer, or per-step overhead are the candidates, and a profile
of one decode step is the next measurement. This is the first architecture
on which the "cards are free, RAM is the whole cost" rule from V4 does not
hold, which is exactly what a second family was for.

**Against the served model.** V4-Flash serves at 26 tok/s with the DSpark
draft and measured 22 without one on the same probes; GLM-5.3-Flash without
a draft is 18.7, 15 % behind at the same quantization family. The
equivalent lever here is the MTP layer (ik PR #2399 reports 1.43× on a
CPU-only box), not yet built.

**Thirteen layers is one too many for `-ub 4096`.** Decode reached 19.6,
then the 11.4k prefill died on the 24 GB card in `ggml_cuda_pool_vmm::alloc`
— the run-time pool for the ubatch had no room next to three expert layers
and the load-time buffers (20.1 GB before the prefill began) — and the
server aborted rather than failing the request, the same class as the V4.1
fork's `-ub 4096` abort (mainline #28338). On this box the ubatch and the
third card-resident layer on the 24 GB card are alternatives; ten layers
(seven plus three) is the standing placement at `-ub 4096`, and the pool
sits at 32.3 / 23.1 GB after a long prefill.

**Without the cards, 8.3 tok/s.** `-ngl 0` puts every weight in RAM (193 GB
resident), and decode lands where the arithmetic said it would: the
attention, shared-expert and head tensors add about 8.5 GB a token to the
4.9 GB of routed experts, and 13.4 GB a token at 145 GB/s is an 11 tok/s
ceiling. Prefill stayed at 283 tok/s because the CUDA backend still runs
large batched matmuls on the cards at `-ngl 0` (9.3 GB of VRAM in use after
the prefill), so a CPU-only prefill was not measured; a pure-CPU number
needs the build without CUDA, which is a different binary.

**Perplexity is not comparable across the two models** — different
tokenizers make "per token" a different unit — so 2.04 against the served
V4-Flash's 2.94 on the same text says nothing about which model is better;
the V4-Flash row is here so that the next GLM file (a different
quantization, or a grafted one) has a same-method baseline, and because the
served model's four-chunk figure had not been recorded before.

**Prefill** at 353 tok/s is 70 % of V4-Flash's 500 on the same ubatch and
the same expert placement (seven plus four there, seven plus three here).

## Threads: 16 is 32

At the standing placement, `-t 16 / 24 / 32` decode 19.0 / 19.0 / 19.1
tok/s (three runs each, spread 0.1). The CPU's only work at `-ngl 99` is
the routed-expert matmuls, and sixteen threads already saturate the memory
bus (the 09-14 bandwidth probe reached 92 % of the cap with eight), so the
other sixteen were idle. Two consequences: the CPU half of a decode step is
bandwidth-bound, not compute-bound, so faster expert kernels would not
help here; and the machine can serve this model with half its cores. On
V4.1-Flash 32 threads was the best setting, because that model's CPU work
includes the engram row lookups — tens of random 4 KB reads a token, some
of them page faults — which are latency and overlap with more threads. The
thread optimum is a property of what the CPU is asked to do per token, not
of the box.

## MTP as the draft: +12 %, and a greedy divergence that was a coin toss

ik PR #2399 (2026-09-02, "complete MTP self-speculative decoding" for
glm5next) is open and conflicts with main in four files; merged here at
hunk level (the graph and pre-gate hunks from the PR, main's qwen4exp
helper and its relaxed quantize assert), built in its own worktree, and
run at the standing placement with `--spec-type mtp:n_max=N`:

| arm | decode tok/s ×3 | drafted / accepted | acceptance | VRAM |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| merged build, no draft | 18.8 / 18.7 / 18.7 | — | — | 28.2 / 18.9 GB |
| `mtp:n_max=1` | 20.4 / 20.4 / 20.4 | 227 / 171 | 75.3 % | 31.3 / 21.8 |
| `mtp:n_max=2` | 20.6 / 20.9 / 20.9 | 266 / 204 | 76.7 % | 31.4 / 21.8 |
| `mtp:n_max=3` | 20.6 / 20.9 / 20.9 | 285 / 212 | 74.4 % | 31.5 / 21.9 |

Three-quarters of the drafted tokens are accepted and the gain is 12 %,
not 75 %, for the same reason DSpark on V4-Flash buys 18 % rather than its
acceptance rate: verifying two tokens at once on a MoE with the experts in
RAM reads up to twice the expert bytes, and the step is bandwidth-bound, so
the tokens-per-byte improves only where the two tokens happen to route to
the same experts. The PR's 1.43× was measured on a CPU-only box where the
attention runs on the same bandwidth budget and gets amortized too. Past
`n_max=2` nothing more is gained; 21 tok/s is where this file sits with its
own draft, against V4-Flash's 26 with DSpark.

The three MTP arms produced one identical answer per prompt ("Rivers have
shaped human history…"), and the merged build **without** a draft gave a
different one ("…shaped the destiny…") on all three prompts. ~~It is the
answer ik main gives without a draft, so the merge (or the PR) changed the
single-token graph.~~ Wrong attribution, struck the same morning: the
back-to-back window (`glm53f-div.sh`, main 7434a014 vs the merged build,
no draft, same flags and placement, two runs each, top-2 logprobs) gave
**byte-identical 400-token answers on both builds** (md5 `0199edc6` ×4),
and both say "destiny". Neither PR #2399 nor the merge changed anything.

What moved the answer was the placement. Token 23 of the answer is a
near-tie: ` the` at −0.683 against ` human` at −0.709, 0.026 nats apart.
Every run at the all-CPU-experts placement or at L6+2 picked ` human`;
every run at L7+3, L10+3 and `-ngl 0` picked ` the`; and the MTP arms at
L7+3 picked ` human` again, because verifying a draft batches two or three
tokens through the graph and the accumulation order differs from the
one-token step. Same file, same greedy decoding, three device splits and
two batch shapes, one 0.03-nat fork. This is the "greedy not reproducible
across batch decomposition" class from the mainline glm5next threads, seen
here without any bug behind it — a reminder that two 400-token answers
differing is not evidence of a changed graph until the logprobs at the fork
are read. The MTP numbers stand, and so does the merged build's plain path.

## Where the 53 ms go

`nsys` on `llama-cli` decoding 200 tokens at the standing placement (the
trace itself costs 8 %: 57.7 ms a token under the profiler against 53
without). The first attempt recorded no kernels — ik runs the graph through
CUDA graphs and the default trace does not descend into them —
`--cuda-graph-trace=node` does. Per token, summed over both cards:

| where | ms / token | what it is |
|---|---:|---|
| GPU kernels, matmul and gemv | 15.1 | reading the on-card weights: 8.5 GB of attention, shared-expert, embedding and head tensors plus ten layers of experts — about 9.7 GB a token, which at the cards' bandwidth is exactly this |
| GPU kernels, attention proper (KDA recurrence, flash attention, indexer argsort) | 1.4 | the linear-attention state update is cheap |
| GPU kernels, everything else (norms, hyper-connection mixes, copies, quantize) | 3.2 | 465 small kernels a token |
| CPU routed experts, derived | ~26 | 35 layers × 8 of 288 experts × 177 GB ≈ 3.8 GB a token at 145 GB/s; not visible to the CUDA trace |
| remainder | ~12 | 224 stream synchronizations and 168 async copies a token — five hand-offs a layer between the cards and the CPU, and between the two cards |

So the half that is not the RAM read is not the KDA attention and not the
CPU compute (the thread sweep already said that): it is the **on-card
weight read** — this model carries 8.5 GB of non-expert weights a token,
which even at 700 GB/s is 12 ms, twice what the same tensors cost on
V4-Flash — and the per-layer hand-off overhead. Two consequences. Moving
experts to the cards buys little because the cards are already busy for
a third of every step. And the V4.1 recipe of raising the on-card tensors
to Q8_0 "for free" is inverted here: the on-card tensors are the GPU-side
cost, and taking them *down* (the unsloth XL file keeps them at Q8_0;
the `mul_mat_vec_q<Q8_0>` kernel alone is 11 ms a token) is the one lever
that reaches decode without touching the RAM experts — a Q4_K attention
graft would be worth about 5–7 ms a token, or +10–15 %, at a perplexity
cost to measure.

CUDA graphs buy nothing on this path: with `GGML_CUDA_DISABLE_GRAPHS=1`
the decode is 56.5 ms a token against 57.7 with graphs, the 2 350 kernel
launches a token costing 7.5 ms of CPU-side API time that overlaps with
the GPU anyway.

## Graft A: the on-card tensors to Q4_K — +10 % decode, 7 % of top-1 tokens change

The breakdown above said the lever on this file is the opposite of the
V4.1 recipe: the tensors that live on the cards are Q8_0 in unsloth's
UD-Q4_K_XL, and reading them is 15 of the 53 ms. So the first graft
requantizes only those. `llama-quantize` on ik main (built in the
worktree) with `--allow-requantize --leave-output-tensor` and a
`--custom-q` rule set of 22 kB: every expert tensor pinned to the type it
already has (the copy path, `quantize &= tensor->type != new_type`),
every f32 tensor pinned to f32, the KDA attention `q/k/v/output`, the MLA
`q_a/q_b/k_b/v_b`, the three shared-expert and the three dense-layer FFN
matrices to Q4_K (358 tensors, 8.0 of the 9.0 GiB of non-expert Q8_0),
and the rest (`ssm_*` gates, hyper-connection `hc_*_fn`, indexer,
`kv_a_mqa`, `nextn`, token embedding, output head — 1.0 GiB) left at Q8_0.
No imatrix. 235 s, 186 → 182.5 GiB; the file's `general.file_type` reads
Q4_K_M, which is cosmetic.

| file, L7+3, `-ub 4096`, same window | decode tok/s ×3 | prefill 11.4k | VRAM at load | PPL 4 chunks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| UD-Q4_K_XL (baseline arm, 09:15) | 18.7 / 18.7 / 18.7 | 354.8 | 28.2 / 18.9 GB | 2.0373 |
| graft A (09:11) | **20.5 / 20.5 / 20.5** | 355.4 | 25.6 / 18.0 GB | 2.0250 |

+9.6 % on decode, the low end of the +10–15 % the breakdown predicted
(the Q8_0 gemv kernel was 11 ms; halving its bytes should save 5–6 ms of
53, and 4.7 ms is what came back). Prefill is unchanged, as it should be
for a compute-bound phase. The four-chunk PPL moved in both directions by
chunk (1.571→1.605, 1.522→1.480, 1.631→1.619, 2.037→2.025), which is what
a ±0.05 estimate looks like when the effect is smaller than that; it
cannot see this graft.

KL divergence can. Twenty chunks of wikitext at c2048, logits of the
original file dumped once (`--kl-divergence-base`, 5.9 GB), then graft A
against them:

| graft A vs UD-Q4_K_XL, 20 chunks | |
|---|---|
| mean KLD | 0.0465 ± 0.0010 |
| median / 90 % / 99 % KLD | 0.011 / 0.106 / 0.552 |
| same top-1 token | **92.9 ± 0.2 %** |
| PPL ratio | 1.0068 ± 0.0026 |

Seven tokens in a hundred change their argmax. For scale, the TF32
finding on V4.1 was a 0.896 → 0.9995 top-1 agreement that was worth an
environment variable; this is a bigger change in the answer distribution
than that, bought for 10 % of decode, and on this evidence it is not a
serving file. It is a bound, and window 3 tightened it from two sides.

## Window 3: an imatrix barely helps, Q6_K halves the gain and quarters the cost

An importance matrix was computed on the original file from wiki.train
(2 MB, 200 chunks of 512 — disjoint from the wiki.test chunks the KLD
reads; 17 min at the all-CPU-experts placement, 809 entries), and the same
Q4_K rules were rerun with it. Then the same 358 tensors were taken to
Q6_K instead, without one. Same 20-chunk KLD against the same base logits;
the Q6_K file also got the decode arm at L7+3.

| graft | on-card tensors | imatrix | file | mean KLD | same top-1 | PPL ratio | decode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| — (UD-Q4_K_XL) | Q8_0 | | 186.0 GiB | 0 | 100 % | 1 | 18.7 |
| A | Q4_K | no | 182.5 | 0.0465 | 92.9 % | 1.0068 | 20.5 (+9.6 %) |
| A-im | Q4_K | yes | 182.5 | 0.0426 | 93.6 % | 1.0124 | (same bytes as A) |
| B | Q6_K | no | 184.3 | **0.0128** | **96.5 %** | 1.0031 | 19.5 (+4.3 %) |

The imatrix recovers a tenth of the KLD and none of the top-1 loss — the
usual "most of the way back" did not happen. That says the loss is not a
calibration problem; Q4_K is simply too coarse for something in this
tensor set. Q6_K on the same tensors keeps 3.5 % top-1 disagreement and
buys 4.3 %, which is the shape of a bandwidth trade with no free lunch in
it: bytes saved and KLD paid track each other. Where the KLD comes from is
the next question, because the set is not uniform — 34 layers of KDA
`q/k/v` feed a recurrent state where a rounding error compounds along the
sequence, while `attn_output`, the shared experts and the dense FFNs do
not. Window 4 split the Q4_K set in two to check, both with the imatrix:

| graft | to Q4_K | GiB moved | mean KLD | same top-1 | KLD per GiB |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A-im | all 358 | 8.0 | 0.0426 | 93.6 % | 0.0053 |
| C | everything but KDA `q/k/v` (`attn_output`, MLA `q_a/q_b/k_b/v_b`, shexp, dense; 244 tensors) | 4.6 | 0.0399 | 93.5 % | 0.0087 |
| D | KDA `q/k/v` only (102 tensors) | 3.4 | 0.0213 | 95.4 % | 0.0063 |

The hypothesis was wrong. The recurrent projections are the *cheaper*
half per byte; the set without them carries almost the whole KLD of the
full graft. The two halves do not add (0.040 + 0.021 > 0.043), so the
error is not a sum of independent tensor errors either. Which of
`attn_output` (1.9 GiB), the shared experts (1.1) or the MLA projections
(0.6) is the expensive one would take another split, and this is where
the graft line stops for now: every variant measured trades KLD for
bytes at a rate that no serving profile here would take, and the
per-expert placement in ExLlamaV3 (below) is the bigger question.

## ExLlamaV3: the per-expert placement engine, first arm

The same day's research found that ExLlamaV3 1.4.3+ places experts
between VRAM and RAM per expert at runtime (`-mcs`), that its open PR
#315 adds precomputed placement profiles measured at 17 → 33 tok/s on
this model (96 GB card, AVX-512 desktop), and that turboderp's 4.05 bpw
EXL3 of GLM-5.3-Flash carries the `mul1` codebook the CPU path needs
(experts uniform 4 bpw, 142 GiB; attention 5–6 bpw, 5.5 GiB — the byte
budget our graft line was chasing, by construction). It was downloaded
(154 GiB, verified) and exllamav3 1.5.0 installed in a venv. First arm,
layer-split mode:

| exl3 1.5.0, `-gs 44,21 -mcl 30 -mct 32`, `-cs 32768` | |
|---|---|
| placement | first 30 of 42 MoE layers' experts on the CPU, 12 on the 48 GB card, 24 GB card at 8.6 GB |
| decode ×3 | 16.9 / 17.5 / 17.5 tok/s |
| prefill 11.4k | 241 tok/s (TTFT 47 s) |
| CPU tier | AVX2 (the 5975WX has no AVX-512) |

Same league as ik's 17.0–18.7 on an AVX2-only CPU, prefill 30 % slower.
The per-expert mode is the question. `-mcs 170` did not fit (118 experts a
layer is 58 GiB); the three arms that did are below.

### Per-expert placement (`-mcs`), dynamic

`-mcs N` keeps `288 − N` experts of every MoE layer resident on the cards
and streams the rest from RAM through the CPU worker; the runtime then
moves experts between the two pools by observed routing (64 experts every
128 steps, per PR #315's description). Same probe, same session, GPUs at
1 MiB before the first arm, `/proc/pressure/io` some avg10 = 0.03, no
other measurement on the box (no lease file was taken — the process list
and `nvidia-smi` were the witness).

| arm, `-gs 44,21 -mct 32 -cs 32768` | experts on cards / layer | decode run 1 / 2 / 3 | decode after the 11.4k prefill (64 tok) | prefill 11.4k | VRAM 48 / 24 GB card |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `-mcs 230` | 58 | 16.9 / 18.1 / 19.6 | 17.0 | 322 | 39.2 / 0.0 |
| `-mcs 210` | 78 | 18.1 / 19.5 / 20.9 | 18.1 | 339 | 43.5 / 7.9 |
| `-mcs 195` | 93 | 19.1 / 20.5 / 22.2 | 19.2 | 356 | 43.8 / 15.2 |
| ik main L7+3 (reference) | 10 whole layers | 18.7 | — | 353 | 32 / 23 |

Two readings. First, the decode climbs run to run in every `-mcs` arm and
does not in the static layer-split arm above (16.9 / 17.5 / 17.5), so the
climb is the placement moving, not CUDA warm-up. Second, the climb is
partly an artefact of the probe: three greedy runs of the same prompt
produce the same 400 tokens, so the routing histogram the runtime
optimises against is that one essay's. When the text changes — the 64
tokens decoded after the wiki prefill — the rate drops back 2–3 tok/s
(22.2 → 19.2 for `-mcs 195`). The steady-state number for a mixed
workload is between those two and needs alternating prompts to measure;
that run is next.

Even the pessimistic column beats the ik rows at a like budget: `-mcs 210`
holds 51 GB of VRAM against ik L7+3's 55 GB and decodes 18.1–20.9 versus
18.7; `-mcs 195` at 59 GB reaches 19.2–22.2. Prefill is now on par with ik
(356 vs 353) rather than 30 % behind, because more of the expert math runs
on the cards. The 3090 was almost idle in the two larger-N arms (0 and
7.9 GB); the 195 arm is the first to use it.

### Mixed text: the steady state a server would see

`exl3-bench2.py` (in `tools/exl3/`) cycles five prompts — the rivers essay,
a Python ISO-8601 parser, a bandwidth explanation, a lighthouse story, a
Rust iterator merge — so the placement never gets to specialise on one
routing histogram. `-mcs 185`, ten runs, 15:07:

| cycle | essay | python | explain | story | rust |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 19.8 | 19.8 | 19.8 | 20.0 | 19.6 |
| 2 | 22.1 | 20.6 | 20.7 | 20.6 | 20.6 |

Prefill 373 tok/s, 19.7 after it. The mixed steady state is **20.6–20.7
tok/s**, not the 23.3 the repeated essay reached — the single-text number
is what the placement can do when the histogram holds still, the mixed
number is what it does when routing keeps changing under it. The one
outlier (22.1 on the essay's second visit) says the placement had been
pulled towards that text by the prefill immediately before it. Against
ik L7+3's 18.7, which does not depend on text, the honest gap is +10 %
draft-free, at 9 GB more VRAM.

A side observation for the reproducibility file: the same greedy prompt
produced different text on its two visits (the essay's second title
changed, the lighthouse changed name). Nothing is sampled; the resident
set differs between the visits, so the same expert sums are accumulated
on different devices in a different order and a near-tie flips. Same
class as the ik `human`/`the` split earlier in this log — placement is
one more axis along which greedy is not reproducible on this engine.

### MTP on ExLlamaV3: slower, at the default depth

The like-for-like against ik's best row (MTP draft, 20.9) is `-mtp`, which
loads the model's own `blk.45` predictor as the drafter. Same window,
15:12–15:18:

| arm | decode, runs 1–4 | drafted accepted / rejected (run 3) | after prefill | prefill | VRAM |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `-mcs 185 -mtp` | 15.3 / 19.0 / 20.4 / 20.6 | 238 / 248 (49 %) | 13.2 | 341 | 45.2 / 20.7 |
| `-mcs 185`, no draft (above) | 19.8 / 21.2 / 23.1 / 23.1 | — | 19.8 | 361 | 43.5 / 20.5 |
| `-mcs 195 -mtp` | 16.3 / 16.8 / 18.9 / 18.0 | 232 / 272 (46 %) | 19.6 | 328 | 45.5 / 15.3 |
| `-mcs 195`, no draft (above) | 19.1 / 20.5 / 22.2 | — | 19.2 | 356 | 43.8 / 15.2 |

The draft costs 2.5–4 tok/s instead of buying the 12 % it bought on ik,
and the acceptance is 46–49 % against ik's 75 % on the same essay with
the same predictor. The two numbers explain each other once the depth is
read from the generator source: exllamav3 drafts **4 tokens a step** by
default (`num_draft_tokens`, `generator.py:169`) where ik saturated at
`n_max=2`. An MTP head predicts one token from the target's hidden state;
chaining it four deep means positions 3 and 4 are predicted from its own
guesses and mostly fail, so half the drafted tokens are thrown away — and
each verify step still reads the expert bytes for five positions with the
experts half in RAM. The 64-token decode after the 11.4k prefill fell to
13.2 for the same reason: cold placement plus a five-wide verify.

This is a setting, not a ceiling: the generator also has
`dynamic_draft_tokens`, which prunes the draft when the predicted
acceptance drops. Depth 1, depth 2 and dynamic are running as this is
written (`exl3-bench3.py` exposes them as `--draft_n` / `--dyn_draft`).

Threads: `-mct 48` on `-mcs 185` gave 19.5 / 20.7 / 22.0 / 22.1 against 32
threads' 23.1–23.3; the CPU side is bandwidth-bound and 32 is the right
number here, as it was for ik (16/24/32 → 19.0/19.0/19.1).

## Things checked and not reported

The loader prints `buffer type overridden` lines for the MTP layer's
expert tensors (`blk.45`) and then reports 29 skipped tensors, 4 367 MiB.
Read against the loader source, the 29 tensors are that layer: it is
skipped whole when MTP is off and the override lines print before the
skip. No RAM is wasted; the log line is merely misleading.
